Our Approach

Most businesses have the same data problems large companies do. What they don’t have is the budget or headcount for the way large companies usually solve them. That gap is what this practice is built for.

01 — The model

Enterprise practices, lean infrastructure.

The engineering standards don't change: version control, tested pipelines, defined metrics, monitoring. What changes is the footprint, and the bill.

Typical enterprise approach Our approach
Platform Licensed BI and ETL suites with per-seat pricing Open-source and managed services; pay for what you use
Infrastructure Dedicated servers or a cloud data platform to administer Serverless and managed hosting, with nothing to patch
People A data team and an IT team to keep it running One accountable builder, plus documentation anyone can follow
Timeline Months of requirements and procurement Weeks, delivered in usable pieces
Ownership Tied to vendors and contracts Your accounts, your code, your data

02 — Principles

Four rules we build by.

They sound simple. They're also the four things most often skipped when a project goes sideways.

01

Start from the decision

Every dashboard and pipeline starts with a question someone needs answered, not with a tool someone wants to use.

02

Use what you already have

We connect to your existing systems rather than asking you to replace them or change how your team works.

03

Trust the numbers

Output is validated against figures you already know, so the first thing you see is something you can believe.

04

Leave it maintainable

Clear code, clear docs, and no clever one-offs, so the next person to touch it (you, us, or anyone else) can pick it up.

03 — The payoff

What good data actually surfaces.

Integrated, well-modeled data shows you things no single system can. A simple retail example: the products that sell the most are often not the ones that make the most money.

Share of volume Share of gross margin

Illustrative example, not client data. Line C earns far more than its volume suggests, while Line A's margin trails its volume. Seeing this takes sales and cost data joined in one model.

The same pattern shows up everywhere: service lines versus billable hours, marketing channels versus customer lifetime value, programs versus cost per outcome. The industry changes; the work doesn't. Connect the data, model it properly, and put the answer in front of the person who needs it.
